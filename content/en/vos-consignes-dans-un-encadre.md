Every salon has three or four sentences everyone must read: when to arrive, the cancellation
policy, parking, what to bring. In the middle of a paragraph they vanish. In a box, they jump out.

## Three callouts, three jobs

cerise offers three kinds of callout. Pick the one that matches the tone of the message.

<Callout type="note">
Arrive ten minutes before your appointment: settling in is part of your time.
</Callout>

<Callout type="astuce">
Parking is free behind the building after 5 p.m.
</Callout>

<Callout type="attention">
Cancellations must be made at least 24 hours in advance.
</Callout>

These examples are made up for the demo. You know your own rules by heart: write them exactly
the way you say them at the front desk.

## How to write one

In the editor, the **Callout** button inserts a block; pick its kind from the list, then write
inside it like any paragraph. In source mode, a callout looks like this:

```
<Callout type="note">
Arrive ten minutes before your appointment.
</Callout>
```

This is called a **named component**: a tag taken from a short list known in advance. cerise turns
it into a callout when you save. A tag that is not on the list is refused, with its name in the
message, so a post can never show a half-broken block.

## One callout per page, usually

If everything is boxed, nothing stands out. Keep the callout for the sentence a client must
remember even if she reads nothing else.
